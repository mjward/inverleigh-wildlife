# Inverleigh bird list: hotspots, gaps, and current threat status

Live check for the Inverleigh wildlife site, centre -38.10, 144.05. Access date for every source below is 25 October 2026. Counts are from the Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) biocache on that date, or from search-indexed eBird pages. Direct requests to ebird.org returned an Anubis bot challenge and were not bypassed, so an eBird page total is cited only when the indexed copy showed it.

The 70-candidate list is the ALA species facet within 10 km (`docs/SPECIES-SOURCES.md`). That facet was re-read on 25 October 2026. Ranks 1–70 and their record counts match the candidate table exactly, from Australian Magpie *Gymnorhina tibicen* (2,450) through White-necked Heron *Ardea pacifica* (170). The cutoff for the top 70 is about 170 records.

ALA `species:"Scientific name"` is the same field as that facet. A name that is only an eBird/Clements synonym can come back as zero even when checklists use it. Where that happened, both figures are given. Distances are great-circle kilometres from -38.10, 144.05 to a sample coordinate on an eBird Australia record for that locality string. A sample point is a checklist coordinate stored by ALA, so it can sit on a track or a centroid rather than on an official pin.

The column `VIC` in the 28 August 2026 national threatened-species CSV means the extract records the taxon in Victoria. It is not a Flora and Fauna Guarantee (FFG) category. FFG categories below come from Species Profile and Threats Database (SPRAT) state-status lines, the indexed March 2025 FFG threatened list, or SWIFFT pages that cite a named FFG list. The July 2026 FFG Excel/PDF on environment.vic.gov.au is the current list, but the file host returned a Cloudflare challenge and no direct file URL was published in the data catalogues, so that workbook was not opened. The July 2026 amendment note on the list page adds Christmas Guinea-flower and a carp threatening process. It does not name any bird.

## Hotspots

Places below are inside about 15 km, ordered from the town outwards. Brisbane Ranges, Clarkesdale, Enfield, Steiglitz, Upper Stony Creek, You Yangs, Meredith, and the Geelong estuary are Golden Plains or Geelong eBird sites and are outside this ring. Barwon River at Winchelsea is about 16.6 km south-west and is listed only as a boundary note.

eBird "Species Leaders" and "Native and Naturalized" figures are species counts. "Checklist Leaders" figures are counts of checklists. ALA figures are distinct scientific names on eBird Australia records for that exact locality string.

## Rare/ID cautions

Australian Raven and Little Raven both occur, and the ratio is large enough to matter. Little Raven *Corvus mellori* is candidate 6, with 1,477 records within 10 km and 2,334 within 15 km. Australian Raven *Corvus coronoides* has 71 records within 10 km (rank 102) and 92 within 15 km: VBA 34, First Bird Atlas 17, eBird 16, Birdata 4. Latest eBird record inside 10 km is 2021. There are 11 eBird records on the Inverleigh Nature Conservation Reserve string and 3 on Bannockburn Bushland. Forest Raven has none. A corvid in this circle is usually a Little Raven, and an Australian Raven still needs a careful call or a good look at the throat hackles. Both species are on the reserve's eBird species facet, so a single-species assumption will hide real records.

Other pairs that are both in the local data:

- Lorikeets. Rainbow Lorikeet (153), Purple-crowned Lorikeet (143), Musk Lorikeet (31 on the accepted name, plus indexed eBird counts under *Trichoglossus concinnus*), and Little Lorikeet (26) all occur. A small green lorikeet in flowering gums is not automatically a Musk Lorikeet.
- Grebes. Hoary-headed Grebe is candidate 69 (172 records). Australasian Grebe has 132 and is missing from the candidate list. Both are on the local wetlands.
- Treecreepers. White-throated Treecreeper is candidate 63 (216). Brown Treecreeper has 15 records and is the threatened one. The common treecreeper on a reserve walk is the White-throated.
- Currawongs. Pied (96) and Grey (50) both occur. Pied is not a Geelong-only bird in this extract.
- Corellas. Long-billed Corella is candidate 29 and Little Corella is candidate 60. Both are common enough to be in the top 70.
- Rosellas. Eastern Rosella is candidate 7 and Crimson Rosella is candidate 54.
- Shrike-tit. Eastern Shrike-tit *Falcunculus frontatus* is candidate 55 (290 records, latest records are current on the Teesdale–Inverleigh Road indexed eBird page, 5 May 2026). The EPBC-listed bird is the northern subspecies *F. f. whitei*, which is not the Victorian bird. See the candidate check.
- Swamphen. Local ALA rows are under *Porphyrio porphyrio*. eBird and field guides often say Australasian Swamphen or *Porphyrio melanotus*. They are the same local bird in this extract. *P. melanotus* as an ALA species name returned 0.
- Musk Lorikeet names. eBird pages say *Trichoglossus concinnus*. ALA's accepted species field says *Glossopsitta concinna*. Counting only one of those names drops the other.
- Wood Duck. Candidate 38 is Australian Wood Duck *Chenonetta jubata*. eBird's name on indexed pages is Maned Duck.
- Black Falcon *Falco subniger* has 65 records within 10 km (rank 108) and 84 within 15 km, latest 2025, sample point about 9.1 km east-south-east. It is scarce relative to Brown Falcon and Nankeen Kestrel, and it is easy to pass over as a dark Brown Falcon. It is also FFG Critically Endangered. See below.
- Hardhead *Aythya australis* has 89 records within 10 km, latest 2025, and is regular at Stephens Road ponds and Murgheboluc. It was removed from the FFG threatened list in June 2024. It is not a current threatened species.
- Shorebird vagrants at Lake Murdeduke, on the 15 km edge, need care. The ALA eBird species facet for the lake string includes Curlew Sandpiper (197 records on that string), plus smaller numbers of Pectoral Sandpiper, Ruff, Long-toed Stint, Red Knot, and Little Stint. Those are lake records. They are not Inverleigh Common records. Curlew Sandpiper is EPBC Critically Endangered.
- Diamond Firetail, Hooded Robin, Speckled Warbler, Painted Honeyeater, and Bush Stone-curlew are the main "people still name them, the recent data do not" group. Their years are in the missing-species table. Listing them as expected residents would overstate what the atlases show after 2015–2019.

## Candidate-list status checks

All 70 scientific names were checked against the 28 August 2026 EPBC threatened-species CSV. A match was counted if the CSV scientific name or current scientific name was the candidate binomial, or started with that binomial plus a subspecies epithet.

None of the 70 candidate binomials is an EPBC threatened taxon. Three CSV rows share a genus and species with a candidate and are different taxa. They do not change the candidate's status.

- *Acanthiza pusilla magnirostris*, King Island Brown Thornbill, Endangered, VIC column "-". Candidate 37 is *Acanthiza pusilla*, the mainland Brown Thornbill.
- *Aquila audax fleayi*, Tasmanian Wedge-tailed Eagle, Endangered, VIC column "-". Candidate 46 is *Aquila audax*.
- *Falcunculus frontatus whitei*, Northern Shrike-tit, Vulnerable, VIC column "-". Candidate 55 is the eastern bird, *Falcunculus frontatus*.

*Platycercus elegans melanopterus* (Kangaroo Island Crimson Rosella, Vulnerable) and *Platycercus caledonicus brownii* (Green Rosella, King Island) are also in the CSV. Candidate 54 is *Platycercus elegans* without a subspecies, and that binomial is not the listed taxon. *Pachycephala rufogularis* (Red-lored Whistler) is a different species from candidate 31 Rufous Whistler *Pachycephala rufiventris* and candidate 66 Australian Golden Whistler *Pachycephala pectoralis*. *Strepera graculina crissalis* is the Lord Howe Island Currawong, not the local Pied Currawong.

The July 2026 FFG workbook could not be downloaded, so this is not a line-by-line FFG clearance of all 70 names from the current Excel file. What was checked:

- No candidate name appears in the indexed bird lines retrieved from the March 2025 FFG threatened list. The birds that did appear from the prompt set (Australasian Bittern, Brolga, Bush Stone-curlew, Hooded Robin, Speckled Warbler, Diamond Firetail) are not in the 70, except that none of those scientific names is a candidate.
- Dusky Woodswallow, the candidate most often suspected, is marked "Not listed" against the February 2024 FFG list in the Goulburn Broken bird book. The same page marks Golden Whistler, Jacky Winter, Common Bronzewing, Spotted Pardalote, Silvereye, and Black-faced Cuckoo-shrike as not listed. Those are candidates 66, 47, 30, 11, 53, and 21.
- Brown Goshawk was queried as both *Accipiter fasciatus* (227 records, the candidate name) and *Tachyspiza fasciata* (0). Neither name is in the EPBC CSV.
- White-throated Treecreeper *Cormobates leucophaea* (candidate 63) is not the EPBC/FFG Brown Treecreeper.
- The six introduced candidates (Common Blackbird, House Sparrow, Common Starling, European Goldfinch, Eurasian Skylark, Common Myna) are not EPBC threatened taxa.
